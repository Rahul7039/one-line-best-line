# One Line, Best Line — candidate drafting and scoring

Brief (from this kit's competition.yaml / RULES.txt): one single line of text,
at most 240 characters, any free and entertaining theme. The judge weighs, in
order: (1) really one line on an entertaining theme, (2) stops the scroll in
its first few words, (3) genuinely entertaining rather than merely clever,
(4) lands in a single sentence with a turn/punch at the end, (5) feels fresh.

Scoring below: Scroll / Entertain / Landing / Fresh / Repeat (would a judge
quote it to someone else?) — each 0–5, total /25.

## Winner: #1 — the cat / business model line

Why it wins:
- **Scroll-stop:** "My cat pays no rent" is absurd inside the first five words —
  the reader is already smiling before the sentence ends.
- **Genuinely entertaining, not merely clever:** it is a concrete, visual
  little scene (rent, broken stuff, 16-hour sleeps), not an abstract aphorism.
- **Single-sentence landing:** three escalating facts build to one turn at the
  very end — "business model" reframes the whole list as admiration, which is
  the punch a judge repeats to someone else.
- **Fresh enough:** cat jokes are common, but the respect-for-the-business-model
  twist is not a stock punchline.
- **Short:** well under the 240-character cap, which also helps on the
  competition's smaller-text tie-break.

Runners-up: #2 (planner — extremely quotable, but a slower, more mundane
opening) and #3 ("hang out soon" fictional series — sharp, but more
clever-observation than scene), then #5 (mantis shrimp — freshest idea,
slightly softer punch).
